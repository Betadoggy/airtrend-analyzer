Location: Heathrow Terminal 2, near Gate A17

Opening Hours: Daily, 5:30 AM to 10:00 PM

Access: All Terminal 2 passengers, no reservation needed

You’ve heard of Duty-Free. Now get ready for Runway-Chic.

Louis Vuitton has officially landed at Heathrow Terminal 2 with more than just monogrammed carry-ons. Say bonjour to a brand-new boutique, “Le Café LV”, opened in partnership with Michelin-starred French chef Cyril Lignac. That’s right because bien sûr, your flight to Frankfurt absolutely requires a crab salad and a croque monsieur in a café shaped like a spacecraft.

In This Post

Bag It, Then Brunch It

Positioned past security near Gate A17, the LV boutique is a glossy temple to travel-ready glamour. While browsing that Keepall Bandoulière or the latest sneakers your bank account isn’t ready for, follow the curved arches into Le Café LV, a sleek, design-forward lounge dishing up buttery viennoiseries and luxe comfort food.

We’re talking:

Croissants that flake like boarding passes

Lobster rolls with First Class attitude

Crab and avocado salad for your 8 AM nutritionist-approved Instagram story

A mango-twist Eton Mess that tastes like summer in the South of France

Designed for Jet-Set Daydreams

The café’s been crafted by architectural hotshot Marc Fornes, with a soft-white sculptural ceiling that feels like it belongs more in a Bond villain’s lair than an airport terminal. Floating above are whimsical “Quetzal Mobiles” by Atelier Oï think oversized butterflies doing yoga mid-air.

It’s open to all Terminal 2 passengers; no reservation, VIP invite, or Platinum Card power move is needed.

LV’s World Domination Plan (Café Edition)

This isn’t just a one-off. Louis Vuitton’s Heathrow café joins a growing list of glam gastronomic pitstops: Osaka, St. Tropez, Paris, and Doha are already on the map. What used to be a fashion house is now slowly becoming an empire of passport-stamped pastry palaces.

So next time you’re transiting through Terminal 2, skip the Pret. Step into Paris, sip like a Riviera socialite and leave smelling faintly of leather and lobster.
