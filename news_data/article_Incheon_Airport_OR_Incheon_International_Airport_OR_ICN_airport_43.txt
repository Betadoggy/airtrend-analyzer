A Seoul MP says North Korea's trash-filled balloons disrupted flights for over 10,000 passengers. (Reuters: Kim Hong-Ji)

Seoul MP Jeong Jun-ho says the trash balloons sent over the border from North Korea disrupted over 10,000 travel plans in the South.

North Korea has sent around 1,000 balloons carrying trash over the border to South Korea, retaliating to South Korean activists sending balloons carrying anti-Kim Jong Un materials northward.

Mr Jeong called on South Korea officials to prevent activists from sending balloons over the border in future.

Link copiedShareShare articleTrash-carrying balloons sent by North Korea across the border last month disrupted more than 100 flights carrying 10,000 passengers, a South Korean lawmaker says.

The North's balloons forced a three-hour halt to flights in and out of South Korea's Incheon airport on June 26, and multiple balloon launches over the past month have required other flights to delay take-offs or landings — or even to divert.

North Korea have sent over 1,000 trash-filled balloons over the border to South Korea. (AP: South Korea Joint Chiefs of Staff)

Citing new data from the transport ministry, Mr Jeong  said 115 commercial jets were disrupted by the balloon launches from late May to late June.

The disruptions reportedly affected more than 10,000 passengers.

At least 15 jets, including long-haul arrivals from the United States, were forced to make alternative landings.

Passengers were then driven to Incheon, causing lengthy delays and inconvenience, Mr Jeong said in a press statement.

Hundreds of passengers on flights from San Francisco, Vancouver and Los Angeles "were slated to land at Incheon International Airport but ended up at Cheongju Airport without knowing what was happening," he said.

Mr Jeong called the disruption "an embodiment of Korea Risk", referring to the term used to describe investor hesitancy over military threats from the North.

Pyongyang has floated more than 1,000 balloons carrying bags of trash into the South, retaliating to activists in the South sending balloons carrying anti-Kim Jong Un materials northward.

The MP urged authorities to prevent activists from sending the balloons into the North.

South Korea cannot sanction activists sending balloons across the border due to a 2023 court ruling that bans it as an unjustifiable infringement on free speech.

Relations between North Korea and South Korea are at one of their lowest points in years.

Pyongyang is reportedly ramping up weapons testing as it draws ever closer to Russia, amid accusations that it is supplying arms to Moscow to be used in the war in Ukraine.

The Centre for Strategic and International Studies explains North Korea sent propaganda leaflets over the border during the Cold War, but opts for trash now because it knows touting its ideology to South Koreans is "laughable".

"However… they should not be taken lightly," the Centre added in their report on Monday.
