Title: Malaysian Airlines Airbus A330 in Pressurization Emergency
Source: Newsweek
URL: https://www.newsweek.com/malaysian-airlines-airbus-330-pressurization-emergency-1917140
--------------------------------------------------

Malaysian Airlines has reported that one of its flights en route to Bangkok on Monday had to make a U-turn back to Kuala Lumpur after experiencing a "pressurization issue."

The Airbus A330, carrying 164 passengers and 12 crew members, initiated an emergency descent despite not reaching an altitude of 8,000 feet, and oxygen masks were not deployed.

The airline said in a statement that the aircraft remained stable and landed safely at Kuala Lumpur International Airport, just over two hours after departure. An investigation into the incident is underway.

"Malaysia Airlines flight MH780 on June 24, 2024, experienced a pressurization issue while en route to Bangkok after departing Kuala Lumpur at 5:59pm," the brief statement said.

"The operating pilots initiated an emergency descent although cabin altitude was not exceeded and passenger oxygen masks were not deployed."

Newsweek has reached out to Malaysian Airlines for further comment.

Malaysian Airlines faced another issue last week when a fire broke out on a Boeing 738 aircraft 15 minutes after departing from Hyderabad, India, bound for Kuala Lumpur, forcing it to return for an emergency landing at Hyderabad's Rajiv Gandhi International Airport.

Separately, a Korean Air flight to Taiwan was forced to return to Incheon Airport, west of Seoul, after a sudden depressurization on the plane, a Boeing 737 Max 8, the transport ministry said Tuesday. The incident, which occurred about 50 minutes after the flight's departure, led to 19 of the 133 passengers being sent to hospitals due to ear pain and nosebleeds, though none suffered serious injuries.

The cause of the depressurization on the Korean Air flight is under investigation. The aircraft has been grounded, and the transport ministry has ordered South Korea's 11 airlines to inspect the pressurization systems on all 400 of their aircraft.

The 737 Max has faced renewed scrutiny over safety. The aircraft crashed in 2018 in Indonesia killing 189 people. Less than 6 months later a 737 Max operated by Ethiopian Airlines crashed only six minutes after departure from the capital city of Ethiopia, Addis Ababa. All 157 people aboard died. After that, the aircraft was grounded worldwide until December 2020 when the company stated they had fixed the issue with MCAS software.
