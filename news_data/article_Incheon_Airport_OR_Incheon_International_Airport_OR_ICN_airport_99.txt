Late last week, South Korea's Justice Ministry lifted a travel ban on Lee Jong-sup, allowing the newly appointed diplomat to depart Seoul despite the highly publicised accusations levelled at him.

The Corruption Investigation Office for High-ranking Officials (CIO) is continuing to examine allegations Mr Lee tampered with a South Korean Marine Corps' inquiry into the death of Lance Corporal Chae Su-geun, who died in July last year during a search mission.

On March 4 the former defence minister was named by President Yoon Suk Yeol's conservative administration to replace current envoy Kim Wan-joong in Canberra, prompting protests from the opposition liberal Democratic Party (DP).

Democratic Party of Korea members protest the departure of Lee Jong-sup at Incheon Airport, March 10 2024. (Munhwa Broadcasting Corporation)

"President Yoon Suk Yeol [helped along] the flight of Lee, who was accused of committing a material crime, by giving him the title of a diplomat," DP floor leader Hong Ihk-pyo was quoted as saying over the weekend.

"The escape to another country by a key suspect in the ongoing probe related to late Corporal Chae, spearheaded by the president, has now become a reality," the opposition MP told party colleagues during a Monday meeting.

The Democratic Party said it would file a criminal complaint to the CIO, accusing South Korea's foreign minister and justice minister of an abuse of power and interfering with the ongoing investigation, while suggesting national agencies helped facilitate Mr Lee's departure to Australia.

Members of the Democratic Party also gathered at Seoul's Incheon International Airport on Sunday night to protest against Mr Lee's appointment before he flew out of the country on board a Korean Air flight.

Lee Jong-sup arrived in Brisbane on Monday. (Reuters: Jeon Heon-Kyun)

Sources have confirmed to the ABC that Mr Lee landed at Brisbane International Airport early on Monday morning, ahead of making his way to Canberra where he will soon present his credentials as South Korea's next ambassador.

According to South Korean media reports, the Presidential Office and Foreign Ministry claimed to be unaware of the travel ban imposed on Mr Lee in January, who was then able to successfully lobby to the Justice Ministry to revoke it.

While the saga has the potential to cause difficulties in the diplomatic relationship between Australia and South Korea, the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade has welcomed Mr Lee's arrival.

"Australia values its important relationship with the Republic of Korea and looks forward to working with ambassador designate Lee in his new role," a Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) spokesperson told the ABC.

"A date for ambassador designate Lee to present credentials will be confirmed in due course in line with usual procedures," the DFAT spokesperson added.

Last May, two months before the death of Corporal Chae, Australia's Defence Minister Richard Marles held talks with his then counterpart Lee Jong-sup at a meeting where the pair agreed to work on enhancing military ties between both nations.

South Korean company Hanwha was two months later selected to construct new Infantry Fighting Vehicles for the Australian Army in a multi-billion-dollar project based in Mr Marles's Victorian electorate.
